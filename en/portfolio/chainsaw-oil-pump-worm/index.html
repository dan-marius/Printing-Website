Worm gear for a chainsaw oil pump: reverse engineering

A replacement part for a customer: I rebuilt the worm gear from the worn part's dimensions, simulated the meshing, tested a PLA prototype, corrected the model and printed the final part in PETG. The pump works again.

Period
October 2026
Role
Design and manufacturing, for a customer
Tools
FreeCAD (Part Design), FDM printing
Materials
PLA (prototype), PETG (final)

Context

The chainsaw's oil pump, which lubricates the chain, is driven through a gear train: the gear wheel receives the motion, and the worm on its hub drives the pump pinion.

On the customer's part, made of white plastic, the worm threads and the gear teeth were worn. Without it, the pump gets no drive and the chain is no longer oiled.

Inputs

  • Photos of the worn original part sent by the customer: the gear wheel with the worm on its hub.
  • Rough dimensions and tooth counts, measured by the customer on the original plastic part and on the part that drives it.
  • The pump pinion that the worm meshes with.
  • The requirement: a replacement that fits in place of the original and withstands friction and operating heat.

The path taken

  1. Analysing the original

    Starting from the customer's rough dimensions, tooth counts and photos, I worked out the geometry: the diameters, the worm's pitch and profile, the hub and the bore.

  2. Modelling in FreeCAD

    I modelled the gear with the integrated worm and, separately, the pump pinion, so I could check the meshing in an assembly.

  3. Simulating the meshing

    I simulated the assembly in FreeCAD: the gear receives the motion, and the worm on its hub drives the pump pinion. I showed the simulation to the customer before printing.

  4. PLA prototype

    I printed a first model in white PLA, quick and cheap, and sent it to the customer for a trial fit.

  5. Corrections after the trial

    The customer confirmed the changes: a thicker gear side and a reversed working direction.

  6. Final part in PETG

    I printed the corrected model in black PETG, which handles friction and heat better than PLA, and checked squareness with a try square. The customer confirmed the pump works, and I also made spare parts for them.

Models tested

PLA

V1 – trial prototype

The first model, printed in white PLA to check the shape, dimensions and meshing quickly.

Tested: fit and meshing at the customer's.

Outcome: two changes: a thicker gear and a reversed working direction.

PETG

V2 – final part

The corrected model, printed in black PETG for friction and heat.

Tested: squareness of the worm to the gear, with a try square.

Outcome: it works: the pump delivers oil again and the chain is lubricated. I also made spare parts for the customer.

Problems and corrections

01

The original worm was worn

Problem. The worm threads on the gear hub were worn down and broken, and the gear teeth showed wear. The part no longer drove the pump.

Correction. I rebuilt the geometry in FreeCAD from the customer's measured dimensions and tooth counts, and also modelled the pump pinion to simulate the meshing before printing.

Result. A complete model of the assembly, ready for a prototype.

02

The prototype gear was too thin

Problem. At the trial fit of the PLA prototype, the gear side turned out too thin for the fit and for the working load.

Correction. I thickened the gear side in the model, keeping the worm and hub unchanged.

Result. The final gear is stiffer and seats correctly.

03

The working direction was reversed

Problem. The prototype showed that the worm worked in the opposite direction to what the pump needs.

Correction. I changed the hand of the worm's helix in the model, to the hand the pump needs.

Result. The pump is driven in the correct direction.

04

PLA is not suited to a part under friction

Problem. PLA is fine for a prototype, but it softens at relatively low temperatures and is not meant for continuous friction, as in a chainsaw gear train.

Correction. I printed the final part in PETG, which handles friction and heat better.

Result. The final part, with squareness checked using a try square. The customer confirmed the chain is getting oil again.
